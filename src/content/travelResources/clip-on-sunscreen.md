The sunscreen that protects you is the one you actually have with you. A bottle sitting in your hotel room does nothing at noon on a boat or halfway up a volcano. So when I travel I carry a small water-resistant SPF 50 bottle with a built-in carabiner and clip it to a belt loop on my pants.

### Why I like it

- **It is always on you:** Because it hangs on your belt loop, reapplying takes ten seconds instead of digging through a bag, which means you actually do it.
- **Carry-on friendly:** These bottles are travel sized (the one linked below is 2 oz), under the 3.4 oz TSA limit for your liquids bag.
- **SPF 50 and water resistant:** Good for the beach, a hike, or a long sweaty day of walking. Equate's SPF 50 Sport lotion, for example, is labeled water resistant for up to 80 minutes.
- **Easy to share:** It is a little noticeable, and sometimes people laugh at the bottle hanging off my pants. That is fine, I like to laugh at myself. More often, though, someone is genuinely thankful, because you end up somewhere with much stronger sun than anyone expected and you are the one with sunscreen.
- **HSA eligible (Americans only):** Broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 15 or higher counts as an HSA and FSA expense, so you can pay for it with pre-tax money. More on that in [Where Each Dollar Goes](/blog/where-each-dollar-goes/#appendix-hsa-eligible-expenses-and-a-note-on-fsas).

### Any brand works

It does not have to be the brand linked below. Walmart's Equate brand sells clip-on travel bottles too, and they are great. Equate's SPF 50 sunscreen in general is a good, cheap, water-resistant option.

### Refilling it

These tiny bottles get expensive, so once mine runs out I refill it from a big bottle of SPF 50. A few rules if you do the same:

- **Refill the sunscreen bottle, not just any container.** Sunscreen is tested for stability in its own packaging. A 2023 study in the *Journal of Cosmetic Science* moved sunscreens into other containers and found that it hurt the formulas' stability (silicone travel bottles did the worst), and recommended buying travel-size sunscreen instead of transferring it. Clear containers are a problem too, since light can break down some sunscreen ingredients. That matters even more for a bottle that hangs in the sun all day.
- **Wash and fully dry the bottle first,** and refill with a similar sunscreen rather than mixing different formulas.
- **Go by the big bottle's expiration date.** Sunscreen is only guaranteed to keep its full strength until the date printed on the package.

### Small tradeoffs

- A 2 oz bottle does not last long. A full-body application takes about an ounce (roughly a shot glass), so expect to refill often.
- Some places, including Hawaii, Key West, and Palau, ban sunscreens containing oxybenzone or octinoxate to protect coral reefs. Check the active ingredients before a beach trip.

### Practical takeaway

A clip-on sunscreen turns reapplying from a chore into a habit, costs very little, and makes you the most popular person in the group when the sun turns out stronger than expected. For your face on everyday city days, I also use a [daily SPF moisturizer](/travel/resources/face-sunscreen/).
