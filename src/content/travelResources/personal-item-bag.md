Budget airlines like Frontier and Spirit make their money on bag fees. The fare looks cheap until you get to the gate and they charge you for a carry-on. The way around it is a bag that fits their free personal item size, and this Narwey duffel is the one I use. It was cheap, it fit under the seat on Frontier with no questions, and it has held up well.

### Why I like it

- **Sized for the free personal item:** It measures about 17.7 x 12.6 x 6.3 inches, just under Frontier's 18 x 14 x 8 inch personal item limit. That means it goes under the seat in front of you and you never pay for a carry-on.
- **Bigger than it looks:** The main compartment is about 25 liters, which is enough for a few days of clothes if you pack light.
- **Folds into its own pouch:** When you are not using it as your main bag, it packs down small. Throw it in your suitcase or backpack as a spare for souvenirs, or for the trip home when your bag is suddenly fuller than when you left.
- **Slides over a suitcase handle:** The front pocket doubles as a trolley sleeve, so it can ride on top of a rolling suitcase instead of hanging off your shoulder.
- **Held up well:** It is made of thick, water-resistant fabric with metal zippers, and mine has survived being shoved under seats and stuffed full.

### Small tradeoffs

- It is a soft duffel with no hip belt or real back support, so it is not something you want to carry for hours.
- Personal item limits vary by airline, and South American budget airlines are often stricter. I learned this the hard way in [Peru](/travel/peru), where my regular carry-on backpack cost me a $50 surcharge. Always check the specific airline's dimensions before you fly.

### Practical takeaway

If you fly budget airlines even once or twice a year, a bag built to their personal item size pays for itself on the first trip. For anything longer than a few days, or anywhere you will be walking a lot with your bag, a proper travel backpack like the [Osprey Farpoint](/travel/resources/gear) is the better choice.
